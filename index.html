Idea 1: How To HumanThe idea

01 · The moment

Life is built from small human moments, like a chat with the neighbour or ringing a friend’s house.

02 · The tension

Tech made life easier, and in the process some of these moments slipped away without us really noticing.

03 · The idea

How to Human: A survey uncovers the ‘state of the nation’ on the very basic human skills we’ve lost. Australians call in about the everyday human moments they’ve lost touch with, using a presenter, we set out to help them get those moments back, learning from the people who never stopped doing them all along.

Idea 1: How To HumanThe tonal shift

Move from ‘how to’ being instructional to ‘how to’ being observational. So, it’s not Youi telling people how to human, it’s us observing how to human, alongside Youi.

From · InstructionalThis is how you do it…
→
To · ObservationalThis is how ‘they’ do it…

Idea 1: How To HumanThe idea mechanic

01

Ask Australia

A Youi survey finds what tech has given us and which human skills we’ve lost and moments we miss.

02

Start the conversation

The findings become earned stories, fronted by a spokesperson (Matt Okine) and seeded to press to build momentum.

03

Open the phone lines

A campaign encourages Aussies to leave Matt a voicemail or voice note about a human moment they need help with.

04

Pick the episodes

One real caller and one relatable problem become the device for Matt to explore in each episode.

05

Find the answer

Matt seeks advice from unexpected people, often a generation or two away from the problem. For example, asking a classroom of five-year-olds how to make friends. Then, putting the advice into practice, we follow Matt as he tests out the tactics he’s been offered by his focus group.

06

Close the loop

Matt calls back the person who left the voicemail in the first place and tells them what he learned.

Each episode lives on YouTube and cuts into shorts, reels, audio clips and organic posts. A Youi webpage houses all the content, building a repository of Matt Okine’s explorations on ‘how to human’, which creates a strong library to build LLM and search visibility.

How to make a new friend

Social media means we’re connected to hundreds of people, yet making a real new friend as an adult feels harder than ever. How to find love

Swiping has made meeting people easier than ever, but somewhere along the way we lost the art of the first move. Matt Okine explores how love used to start, learning from couples who’ve been together 50 and 60 years, and finds out whether their old ways still work today.

Episode 03

How to make small talk

With our phones always within reach, a chat with a stranger has become something we’d rather avoid. Matt Okine explores the lost art of small talk where it still happens most: the back of a rideshare.

Episode 04

How to meet your neighbours

We can see what’s happening on the other side of the world, but many of us don’t know who lives next door. Matt Okine explores how neighbours used to become friends, finding the person who knows everyone on their street. Then he knocks on a few doors of his own.

Idea 1: How To HumanThe role of Youi

Idea 2: Connection Experiment (Lite)The idea

01 · The moment

As technology has become part of everyday life, it’s slowly taken us further away from some of life’s simple human moments.

02 · The tension

The further these moments drift from everyday life, the bigger we build them up in our heads. Every time we avoid one, the gap grows, until the connection we crave becomes the thing we avoid.

03 · The idea

Connection Experiment: we ask Australians what they believe about human connection, then put four of those beliefs to the test over 24 hours, closing the gap between what we expect and what actually happens.

Idea 2: Connection Experiment (Lite)The idea mechanic

01

Ask Australia

A Youi survey surfaces the tensions worth testing: the everyday human moments we now rely on tech for, and the ones we’d struggle with without it.

02

Start the conversation

The findings become earned stories, cut by generation and seeded to press to build momentum before episode one.

03

Set the challenges

The sharpest tensions become four challenges, each framed as “What happens when…?”, like a date, a trip across town and making a new friend.

04

Creator-led content

A narrator follows four creators from different stages of life as each takes on one challenge, with no tech to guide them.

05

Document it

Using a mix of shot content and the creators’ own footage, we focus on what unfolds between people: the build-up, the moment itself and reflections from both sides.

06

Hand over

We share what the four creators found and ask whether it’s true for everyone, which can be the lead into the Connection Experiment.

There’s no formal measurement in March. That’s reserved for the full Connection Experiment.

Idea 2: Connection Experiment (Lite)Tonality

Idea 2: Connection Experiment (Lite)Connection Experiment (Lite)

We use creators and set them challenges that take away technology to get back to the heart of human connection. Over four episodes we set the challenges, and through a combination of shot content and UGC from the creators, we document them attempting to navigate moments of human connection without technology to guide them. Each episode asks “What happens when…?” and focuses on what unfolds between people.

Episode 01

What happens when you go on a date with no tech?

Younger content creator

The challenge: Go on a date with no tech.

We follow a Gen Z content creator tasked with organising and going on a date without the use of any technology. Together with them, we capture the lead-in to the date, the date itself and then reflections from both the creator and their date about how the experience was.

Episode 02

What happens when a family crosses town without GPS?

A family: mum, dad and kids (ages like 7 and 9)

The challenge: Make it across town to some kind of kids’ playground without technology (no GPS, just a Refidex).

We capture a family of four as they make their way across town to a destination they’ve never visited before (an indoor play centre, a movie theatre on the other side of town etc.). A car rigged with GoPros captures every missed turn-off, every heckle from the back seat and the moment they make it there.

Episode 03

What happens when a dad has to make a new friend at a kids’ party?

A dad with a young kid

The challenge: Make a connection with someone at a party and make a plan to do something together at a later date (a hit at the driving range, a play date with the kids etc.).

We send a dad and his 4-year-old to a kids’ birthday party, tasked with meeting a new friend and making a plan with them. Leading into the party we capture his thoughts, feelings, game plan and the perspective of his partner or child, as well as how it goes on the ground. After the fact we check in with him to uncover how he found the experience and if he was successful in making a new mate.

Idea 2: Connection Experiment (Lite)WWW

The hub

  • Campaign hub. One home for all four challenges, including every episode, the creators and how to take part.
  • Challenge pages. Each one has the episode, transcript, behind the scenes, the creator’s diary and both sides’ reflections. Each page is built around the search behind the moment, like “how to make friends as an adult” or “date ideas without apps”.

For audiences

  • Take the challenge. Downloadable challenge cards so people can try a no-tech date, trip or party themselves.
  • Share your story. People upload their own attempts or stories, and the best are shared with their permission.
  • Meet the creators. A short profile for each creator, with links to their channels.
  • Nominate someone. People suggest the friend or family member who’d struggle most without their phone.
  • Be part of what’s next. Register interest for the Connection Experiment, which builds the list of participants and audience before it launches.
  • Subscribe and get notified. Episode alerts by email or SMS.

For search and AI

  • Questions page. The real questions behind each challenge, each with a short answer.
  • Expert notes. A short take on each challenge from a social psychologist or relationship expert, which makes the content more credible and easier for AI tools to cite.
  • Media room. Episode assets, stills, creator bios and any stats for journalists.
  • Built properly for search. Transcripts, captions and the right page setup so search engines and AI tools can read and cite it.
